# Phase 2: User research

**Goal:** Capture everything the team knows about the real users: who they are, how
they work today, what frustrates them, and the conditions they work in.

**Done when:** Every user group has goals, context, current process and pain points
recorded, and each finding is tagged with how it is known.

## Important framing

The person running the session is not the end user. What they tell you is the
team's knowledge, which may be accurate or may be a belief. Tag every finding with
its evidence level:

- **Observed**: they watched users do it, or have data (logs, tickets, analytics).
- **Reported**: users told them directly.
- **Believed**: the team's opinion or guess.

Anything **Believed** that later decisions depend on becomes an assumption with a
matching entry in `user-questions.md`.

## Question areas (repeat for each user group)

**Who they are**
- Job title and responsibilities. Where this product fits in their day.
- Tech comfort (low, medium, high), with an example of tools they already use well.
- Languages they read comfortably.

**Context**
- Where they use it: desk, shop floor, warehouse, car, customer site.
- Device and screen size, input method (mouse, touch, scanner, keyboard only).
- Conditions: noise, lighting, gloves, interruptions, time pressure.
- Connectivity in those places.
- Frequency of use (several times a day, weekly, monthly) and session length.

**Current process**
- How they do this task today, step by step. Ask for a real example.
- Tools and workarounds used today (spreadsheets, phone calls, email, WhatsApp).
- Who else they depend on or hand off to.

**Pain points and goals**
- The three biggest frustrations today, each with an example.
- What "a good day" looks like for them with this task.
- What they are afraid of (making mistakes, being blamed, losing data).
- What information they look for most often.

**Accessibility and inclusion**
- Any known users with vision, hearing, motor or cognitive needs.
- Older users, occasional users, or first-time users.

**Evidence**
- For each key finding: how do you know? (Observed, reported or believed.)
- Is any research, data or support ticket history available to read?

## Traps to probe

- "Users want X": ask which users, and how the team knows.
- Only describing power users: ask about occasional and first-time users.
- Describing the ideal process instead of the real one: ask what happens when
  something goes wrong today.

## Spec section 2 structure

2.1 User groups overview · 2.2 Findings per group (context, current process, pain
points, goals), each tagged Observed/Reported/Believed · 2.3 Key insights (what the
findings mean for the design) · 2.4 Research gaps (linked to user questions)
